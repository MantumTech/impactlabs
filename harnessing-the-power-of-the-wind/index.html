Kit Description

This kit helps students explore how electricity can be created from movement — just like real wind turbines that turn the wind's power into clean energy. By learning how a simple motor can generate electricity, students see how renewable energy works in action.

Green energy, such as wind and solar power, is important because it reduces pollution, protects the planet, and helps build a sustainable future. Every small experiment like this shows how science and creativity can work together to power the world responsibly.

Imagine a small rural village far from the city. The people there don't have access to reliable electricity — when the sun goes down, homes become dark, and students can't study at night. Fuel-powered generators are expensive and create pollution, so the community needs a cleaner, more sustainable solution.

Your mission is to design a small wind turbine that can turn wind energy into electricity using simple materials. By experimenting with your motor and understanding how movement can generate power, you'll explore how renewable energy could bring light and opportunity to communities that need it most.

Safety Rules

⚠️

Important — Read Before Starting

  • Do not short the 9V battery terminals together; this can cause heat and damage.
  • LEDs are bright — avoid staring directly into the light.
  • Keep small parts away from mouths (choking hazard).
  • Keep long hair, fingers, and loose clothing away from any spinning or moving parts.
  • Disconnect the battery when not in use to prevent overheating.

Vocabulary

Circuit
A closed loop path for electricity.
Series
Components in a single path; current flows through each in turn.
Polarity
Direction-sensitive parts (battery, LED) have a + and − side.
Resistor
Limits current, so components don't burn out.
Switch
Opens (off) or closes (on) the circuit.
Generator
A device that converts mechanical energy (motion) into electrical energy.
Motor
A device that converts electrical energy into mechanical motion.
Mechanical Energy
Energy from movement or motion.
Electrical Energy
Energy from the flow of electric current.
Power Source
Provides electrical energy to run the circuit.

LED Lesson

Lesson 1

Purpose

Students build a simple flashlight to learn about power sources, polarity, buttons, resistors, and basic circuit flow.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify battery positive/negative and LED anode/cathode.
  • Explain why an LED needs a resistor in series with a battery.
  • Assemble a series circuit with a button that controls an LED.

Materials

  • 1× 9V battery with snap connector leads
  • 1× LED (red recommended for first build)
  • 1× Resistor 470 Ω (acceptable: 330–1k Ω)
  • 1× Push button or toggle switch (SPST)
  • ~6× Jumper wires (male–male for breadboard)
  • 1× Breadboard (400–600 tie points recommended)
  • Opt Tape, small cardboard tube (flashlight body), marker labels

Build Steps

What is a Breadboard?

A breadboard is a small plastic board used to build and test electrical circuits without soldering. It has many little holes where you can plug in wires and components like LEDs or resistors. Inside the board, metal strips connect certain holes together so electricity can flow between them. It's like a reusable puzzle that lets you easily create, test, and change circuits.

Prepare the Battery

Connect the 9V snap to the battery (red = +, black = −). Keep the battery disconnected from the circuit while building.

Identify LED Legs

  1. Long leg = anode (+)
  2. Short leg = cathode (−)
💡
If legs are trimmed and you can't tell, the flat side of the LED rim is the cathode.

Place LED on the Breadboard

Insert the LED so each leg sits in a different row (not the same row). Each leg must be in a separate electrically connected row.

Place the Resistor

  1. Connect one end of the resistor into the same row as the LED's anode (long leg).
  2. Put the other end of the resistor into a different row (this will be the row we connect to the battery +).

Wire Battery + to the Resistor

Insert the positive jumper cable into the same row as the resistor.

Connect LED Cathode to Battery −

Insert the battery's negative (black) jumper wire into the same row as the LED's cathode.

Final Check

  1. Verify resistor is in series with the LED anode; LED cathode goes to negative — no short circuits (no red directly to black).
  2. Ensure LED orientation: long leg → resistor → +9V; short leg → ground.
9V(+) → [Resistor] → [LED +] → [LED −] → 9V(−)

Connect the Battery

Plug the 9V battery into the snap. The LED should light up!

Troubleshooting

LED reversed? Flip the LED legs in the breadboard.
Loose connections? Re-seat wires and parts firmly into the breadboard.
Bad battery? Measure or replace — a weak 9V may not light the LED brightly.
LED burnt out? If no resistor was used, the LED may be damaged. Replace LED and use a resistor.

Adding a Button

Lesson 2

Purpose

Build on the LED circuit by adding a push button so the LED only lights when pressed — just like a real flashlight.

How a Tactile Switch Works

A typical tactile switch has four pins — but they're actually two pairs of connected pins:

[1] [2] Pins 1–2 are always connected [3] [4] Pins 3–4 are always connected
  • When you press the button, the two sides connect (1–2 connect to 3–4).
  • One side connects to the battery's positive voltage, the other to the LED circuit.
ℹ️
If your button has just 2 pins, it's even simpler — they connect when pressed.

Build Steps

Start with Your LED Circuit

Keep the working LED circuit from Lesson 1 on your breadboard.

Place the Button on the Breadboard

Push the button so that its legs straddle the center gap of the breadboard. This ensures that each side of the button is on a separate circuit row.

Re-wire the Circuit with the Button in Series

Now wire it like this:

  1. Battery positive (red wire) → goes to one side of the button (one row).
  2. Other side of the button → connect a jumper wire from that row to one end of the resistor.
  3. Resistor → connect to LED anode (long leg).
  4. LED cathode (short leg) → connect to battery negative (black wire).
9V(+) → [Button] → [Resistor] → [LED →|] → 9V(−)

Double-Check

  • Button straddles center gap
  • The resistor is still in series with the LED
  • Long leg (anode) of LED connects toward + side
  • No loose or touching wires

Connect the Battery & Test

  • Attach the 9V snap to the battery.
  • Press the button — the LED should light up!
  • Release it — the LED turns off.

Motor Lesson

Lesson 3A

Purpose

Students build a simple fan circuit using a DC motor to learn about power sources, polarity, switches, and how electrical energy can be converted into motion.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify the positive and negative sides of a battery and motor.
  • Explain how a DC motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.
  • Assemble a simple circuit with a switch that controls the motor.
  • Observe how reversing the polarity changes the motor's spin direction.

Materials

  • 1× 9V battery with snap connector leads
  • 1× Small DC motor (3–9V range)
  • 1× Push button or toggle switch (SPST)
  • ~6× Jumper wires (male–male for breadboard)
  • 1× Breadboard (400–600 tie points recommended)
  • Opt Small paper or plastic fan blade, tape, marker labels

Build Steps

Identify Motor Terminals

Your DC motor has two metal terminals:

  • One terminal = positive (+)
  • The other = negative (−)
🔄
If you reverse them, the motor spins the opposite direction — polarity just controls spin direction.

Place the Motor

Insert each of the motor's two terminals into different rows of the breadboard. Make sure the motor is secure and the terminals don't touch each other.

Connect the Battery Wires

Use the battery snap wires:

  • Red wire (positive) → insert into the same row as the motor's positive terminal.
  • Black wire (negative) → insert into the same row as the motor's negative terminal.

Connect the Battery

Snap the 9V battery into the clip. The motor should spin!

Reverse Direction (for fun!)

To make the motor spin the opposite direction: swap the battery wires on the breadboard — red ↔ black (reverse polarity).

🏆

Challenge

Can you add a button to the motor circuit to control when it spins? (Hint: use what you learned in Lesson 2!)

Wind Turbine

Lesson 3B

Purpose

Students use a DC motor as a simple generator to learn how mechanical energy can be converted into electrical energy. They will see how spinning the motor's shaft produces electricity that can power a small load, such as an LED.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain how a DC motor can also work as a generator.
  • Describe the relationship between motion (mechanical energy) and electricity (electrical energy).
  • Build a simple circuit where a motor generates power to light an LED.
  • Observe how spinning the motor faster increases voltage and brightness.

Materials

  • 1× Small DC motor (3–9V type — same as before)
  • 1× LED (red works best; it lights at ~2V, lower than blue/white)
  • 1× Breadboard
  • 2× Jumper wires
  • 1× Fan blade, propeller, or small wheel to spin the motor shaft

Build Steps

🔬

Key Concept

A DC motor and a generator are the same device used in reverse. When you apply voltage, the motor spins. When you spin the shaft, it produces voltage and current. This generated electricity can light an LED — if you spin it fast enough!

Prepare Your LED

Identify LED legs:

  • Long leg = positive (anode)
  • Short leg = negative (cathode)

If using a breadboard, insert the LED so each leg is in a separate row.

Connect the Motor to the LED

Use jumper wires or alligator clips:

  • Motor terminal A → LED anode (long leg)
  • Motor terminal B → LED cathode (short leg)

That's it — no battery! The motor itself will generate the power.

Attach a Fan Blade or Propeller

Attach a small fan blade, propeller, or wheel to the motor shaft so you can spin it easily.

Spin the Motor!

  • Spin the shaft quickly by blowing on the fan.
  • The LED should flash or glow faintly at first.
  • The faster you spin, the brighter it gets!
💡
If the LED doesn't light up, try reversing the LED leads — the polarity of the generated voltage might be flipped.
